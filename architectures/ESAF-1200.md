# ESAF-1200 Reference Architecture

**Document ID:** ESAF-1200

**Status:** Working Draft

**Version:** 0.4-alpha

## 1. Purpose

ESAF-1200 establishes the architecture method used to design, select, tailor, document, review, and assess enterprise AI capabilities. It provides vendor-neutral patterns, logical trust zones, control points, decision records, and evidence expectations.

## 2. Scope

ESAF-1200 applies to architecture patterns and capability designs for AI platforms, applications, models, data services, agents, integrations, infrastructure, operations, and assurance. It applies across cloud, on-premises, edge, hybrid, managed-service, and externally hosted deployment models.

ESAF-1200 does not prescribe products, replace detailed engineering standards, or assert compliance with an external framework.

## 3. Relationship to other ESAF publications

- ESAF-1000 defines enterprise management-system and operating-model requirements.
- ESAF-1100 defines control objectives and assessable controls.
- ESAF-1200 defines architecture methods and reusable design patterns.
- ESAF-1400 provides implementation guidance and product-specific examples.
- ESAF-1500 defines assessment methods.
- ESAF-1600 maintains external-standard mappings.

When publications differ in level of detail, the more specific applicable requirement governs. Architecture patterns shall not weaken an applicable control or management-system requirement.

## 4. Normative terms

The terms **shall**, **should**, and **may** have the meanings defined in the ESAF editorial style guide. Pattern content is normative only when explicitly designated as required.

## 5. Architecture governance

The organization shall:

1. assign authority for approving architecture patterns, capability designs, deviations, and retirement;
2. maintain current architecture records for in-scope AI capabilities;
3. select and tailor patterns according to documented risk and operating context;
4. record material decisions, alternatives, consequences, and inherited responsibilities;
5. review architecture upon material change and at a risk-determined interval;
6. preserve evidence supporting design approval and operating responsibility.

Architecture governance shall integrate with the `GOV`, `RSK`, and `ARC` control families.

## 6. Required architecture views

A capability design shall include views sufficient for reviewers to understand:

- business context, actors, intended outcomes, and affected parties;
- systems, services, providers, and responsibility boundaries;
- identities, authorization paths, privileged operations, and delegated authority;
- data, instructions, context, model artifacts, outputs, memory, and telemetry flows;
- trust zones, boundary crossings, enforcement points, and external exposure;
- deployment, integration, availability, recovery, safe-state, and retirement behavior;
- monitoring, evidence, administration, and incident-response paths.

Views may be combined when readability is preserved. Diagrams shall be accompanied by prose describing scope, assumptions, and material flows.

## 7. Pattern selection

Each capability shall select one primary architecture pattern and any supporting patterns required by its design. Selection shall consider purpose, capability tier, risk classification, data classification, autonomy, external exposure, deployment model, model source, provider responsibility, jurisdiction, and applicable obligations.

Selection and tailoring shall follow [`PATTERN_SELECTION.md`](PATTERN_SELECTION.md).

## 8. Trust zones and boundary crossings

Capability designs shall map components and flows to the logical zones in [`TRUST_ZONES.md`](TRUST_ZONES.md). The mapping shall not be represented as a required network topology.

For each material crossing, the design shall identify identities, authorization, data and instruction classifications, validation, policy enforcement, cryptographic protection, secrets handling, logging, correlation, failure behavior, and shared-responsibility changes.

## 9. Required control integration

Patterns shall identify applicable control objectives, base controls, inherited controls, capability-specific controls, and organization-defined parameters. Capability designs shall document where each required control is enforced and what evidence demonstrates design implementation.

A pattern reference cannot replace control applicability analysis. Applicable controls remain governed by ESAF-1100.

## 10. Shared responsibility and inheritance

When a provider, platform, or shared service performs a control function, the architecture shall document:

- the control outcome supplied;
- configuration and operational responsibilities retained by the consumer;
- evidence available from the provider;
- assumptions, limitations, and failure dependencies;
- notification, continuity, portability, and exit arrangements.

Inherited controls shall be verified before reliance and re-evaluated when the provider, service, contract, or architecture materially changes.

## 11. Architecture decisions and deviations

Material choices and deviations shall be recorded through the architecture decision process. A decision record shall identify context, alternatives, decision, consequences, owner, affected controls, required evidence, review triggers, and supersession.

A pattern deviation shall not be treated as an approved exception unless it also satisfies ESAF governance and risk-acceptance requirements.

## 12. Failure and resilience

Designs shall document foreseeable technical, operational, supplier, data, model, human, and adversarial failure modes. Risk-proportionate safeguards shall address timeout, retry, isolation, fallback, safe state, rollback, recovery, emergency suspension, and retirement.

Automated retry or failover shall not bypass authorization, duplicate consequential actions, or transfer information to an unauthorized provider or jurisdiction.

## 13. Observability and evidence

Architectures shall provide protected, attributable, and time-correlated evidence sufficient to operate, investigate, assess, and improve the capability. Evidence design shall cover material input, retrieval, model, output, tool, approval, change, administrative, policy, and failure events as applicable.

Evidence collection shall respect data minimization, access, retention, privacy, confidentiality, and legal requirements.

## 14. Pattern conformance

A capability may claim conformance with a published pattern only when it:

- identifies the pattern identifier and version;
- satisfies required pattern elements or records approved deviations;
- documents selected variants, overlays, parameters, and inherited controls;
- provides the required architecture views and evidence;
- has completed the organization's architecture review.

Pattern conformance does not establish ESAF-1000 conformance, external compliance, certification, safety, or fitness for purpose.

## 15. Pattern lifecycle

Patterns use the states proposed, draft, approved, published, deprecated, and retired. Published identifiers shall not be reused. Deprecation shall identify a replacement or explain why none exists. Retirement shall preserve records needed to interpret historical designs and assessments.

## 16. Maintenance

ESAF-1200 shall be reviewed when material technology, threat, regulatory, deployment, provider, or control changes affect the architecture method or pattern library. Changes to the common contract require an architecture decision and review of published patterns for continued consistency.
