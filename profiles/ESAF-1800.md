# ESAF-1800 Profile Contract

**Status:** Working Draft
**Version:** 0.1.0

## Purpose and scope

ESAF-1800 defines the normative contract for versioned ESAF profiles. A
profile tailors the application of the ESAF library to a stated operating
context through control selections, additional risks, overlays, evidence
expectations, and lifecycle-aware external references. It is an ESAF
implementation artifact and does not state legal requirements or external
scheme requirements.

An ESAF-1800 profile shall preserve the meanings of [ESAF-1000](../framework/ESAF-1000.md)
and [ESAF-1100](../controls/ESAF-1100.md). It shall reuse the shared assessment
semantics of [ESAF-1500](../assessment/ESAF-1500.md) and shall treat mappings
published under [ESAF-1600](../crosswalks/ESAF-1600.md) as governed reference
artifacts rather than imported outcomes.

## Profile identity and lifecycle

Each profile shall have a stable, versioned identifier, profile version, and
schema version. The first identifier segment is the profile domain, which
identifies the jurisdiction, industry, sector, or risk context. The reusable
identifier format is
`<profile-domain>--<profile-name>--<major>.<minor>.<patch>`. The reusable
lifecycle vocabulary is `proposed`, `draft`, `approved`, `published`,
`deprecated`, and `retired`.

The United Kingdom pilot identifier is `uk--jurisdiction-profile--0.1.0`; its
profile version and schema version are `0.1.0`, its lifecycle is `draft`, and
its target ESAF release is `v0.5-beta`. Reusable ESAF-1800 artifacts shall
accept other valid profile identifiers and lifecycle states from the closed
vocabulary.

A profile change shall retain a change history identifying the changed version,
date, author, and description. A profile shall not silently change the scope,
selection, evidence basis, or lifecycle representation of an earlier version.

The profile manifest shall identify each required component by a
package-relative component path resolved beneath the versioned package root.
Each component's `$schema` value shall be a document-relative schema locator
resolved from that component document.

## Source and authority boundaries

Each profile shall identify permitted and excluded sources and shall
distinguish original ESAF synthesis from legal, regulatory, or external-scheme
assertions. Additional risks and overlays shall be original ESAF synthesis.
A profile shall not represent an excluded source as governing or supplying a
profile selection.

## Applicability and system boundary

The United Kingdom pilot applies to AI systems deployed or operated in the
United Kingdom, regardless of organizational domicile. An organization shall
record evidence for the United Kingdom deployment or operating basis; domicile,
incorporation, or headquarters alone shall not determine applicability.

The assessed system boundary shall include the AI system and business purpose,
included and excluded components, supporting infrastructure and services,
suppliers and shared-responsibility boundaries, applicable condition answers
and supporting evidence, and known limitations or unresolved scope questions.
The profile does not define the scope of any legal, regulatory, or external
assurance regime.

## Control selections

A profile shall contain exactly one selection for every control in the
authoritative ESAF control catalog. Each selection shall identify its control,
status, and profile rationale. The closed selection vocabulary is
`required`, `conditional`, `recommended`, and `not_selected`.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `required` | Selected for every system within the profile's stated scope. |
| `conditional` | Selected only when one or more referenced applicability conditions resolve to their activating values. |
| `recommended` | Profile guidance expressed with `should`; it cannot affect ESAF conformance. |
| `not_selected` | The profile adds no selection for the control and does not declare the underlying ESAF control inapplicable. |

A `conditional` selection shall reference one or more defined applicability
conditions. Each condition shall have a stable identifier, a factual question,
the boolean answer type, an `activates_when` value, and resolution evidence.
Other selection statuses shall not carry activation conditions. A profile shall
not classify a selection as a legal obligation or as an external certification
requirement.

## Additional risks and overlays

A profile may define additional risks and overlays that strengthen
implementation for its stated context. Each additional risk shall have a stable
identifier, bounded risk statement, in-scope circumstances, source basis,
affected ESAF controls, and one or more mitigating overlays.

Each overlay shall have a stable identifier; a requirement or recommendation;
universal or condition-activated applicability; affected controls; related
risks; related evidence expectations; and an explanation of how it strengthens
implementation. An overlay shall not alter or weaken a core control. Profile
language shall be original ESAF synthesis and shall not reproduce external
requirement text as if it were profile content.

## Evidence and assessment

Profiles shall reuse the evidence-quality attributes, assessment methods,
determinations, time-boundary rules, finding semantics, and maturity levels in
[ESAF-1500](../assessment/ESAF-1500.md). Each evidence expectation shall have
a stable identifier, an evidence purpose and expected artifact class, related
controls or overlays, applicable quality attributes, and any activating
condition. It shall distinguish collected evidence from an assessment
determination.

A profile may strengthen evidence expectations or maturity criteria, but shall
not weaken cumulative maturity prerequisites, calculate an average maturity
score, treat maturity as conformance, or infer an unassessed level. A profile
shall not define a profile-local replacement maturity scale.

## External references

An external reference shall identify its exact registry identifier,
repository-relative registry path, expected lifecycle status, permitted use,
and whether qualified review is required. Its use shall be
`lifecycle_reference_only` unless an ESAF requirement explicitly defines a
different governed use.

Each external reference shall state that relationships, external outcomes, and
evidence are not imported into the profile. A validator shall resolve the
reference against its registry and reject identifier, path, or lifecycle drift.
A referenced artifact lifecycle transition shall require an explicit profile
update before the new state is relied upon. The transition shall not change the
profile lifecycle automatically, and neither artifact shall be represented
beyond its independently governed recorded state.

## Traceability and validation

Profile traceability shall form a closed graph: each additional risk shall
resolve to overlays; each overlay to affected controls and evidence
expectations; each evidence expectation to controls or overlays; each
conditional selection or overlay to defined applicability conditions; each
control to the authoritative catalog; and each external reference to its
registry record.

Validation shall check the profile identity and lifecycle, completeness of
control selections, condition activation, closed traceability, ESAF-1500
reuse, and lifecycle-aware external references. A profile result shall record
the profile identifier, version, lifecycle status, scope, evidence, and known
limitations so that its applicability can be assessed for its stated period.

## Non-claim boundaries

An ESAF-1800 profile shall not claim or imply legal sufficiency, compliance,
certification, equivalence, endorsement, external approval, or production
readiness. It shall not infer an external outcome from an ESAF control,
selection, overlay, assessment result, or external reference. Profile use does
not establish conformance outside the recorded scope, evidence, and assessment
period.
