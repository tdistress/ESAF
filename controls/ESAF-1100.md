## 1. Purpose

ESAF-1100 defines the control architecture used to implement and assess ESAF-1000. It establishes control families, objectives, identifiers, required metadata, assessment methods, evidence expectations, enhancements, baselines, and mapping semantics.

This version defines the catalog structure. It does not yet establish a complete control baseline or claim that every objective has approved controls.

## 2. Control architecture

ESAF uses the following hierarchy:

```text
ESAF principle
  -> control family
    -> control objective
      -> base control
        -> control enhancement
          -> assessment procedure
            -> evidence and metrics
```

### 2.1 Principles

Principles state durable expectations established by ESAF-1000. They guide interpretation but are not independently assessed as controls.

### 2.2 Control families

Families group related controls by enterprise capability. A family is not a maturity level, implementation team, or organizational boundary.

### 2.3 Control objectives

Objectives define the outcome achieved by one or more controls. Objectives use a family prefix and two-digit sequence, such as `IAM-01`.

### 2.4 Base controls

Base controls contain one primary, assessable requirement. Controls use a family prefix and three-digit number, such as `IAM-100`. Numbers increment by ten to preserve space for future controls.

### 2.5 Control enhancements

Enhancements add rigor, scope, automation, assurance, or specialized safeguards to a base control. Enhancements do not replace the base control. They use parenthetical numbering, such as `IAM-100(1)`.

### 2.6 Assessment procedures

Assessment procedures state how an assessor determines whether a control is designed and operating effectively. Procedures use the control ID plus `-A` and a sequence, such as `IAM-100-A1`.

## 3. Identifier rules

### 3.1 Reserved patterns

| Object | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Family | Three uppercase letters | `IAM` |
| Objective | `FAM-NN` | `IAM-01` |
| Control | `FAM-NNN` | `IAM-100` |
| Enhancement | `FAM-NNN(N)` | `IAM-100(1)` |
| Assessment step | `FAM-NNN-AN` | `IAM-100-A1` |

### 3.2 Stability

Published identifiers shall not be reused for a different meaning. A retired identifier remains reserved. Editorial changes that do not materially change the requirement retain the identifier. A materially different requirement receives a new identifier and records its predecessor.

### 3.3 Number allocation

Control numbers are allocated in increments of ten. Draft authors shall obtain an unused number before opening a proposal. Numbering order does not imply priority, maturity, or implementation sequence.

## 5. Required control content

Every proposed base control shall include:

- stable ID and title;
- family and objective;
- lifecycle status and catalog version;
- normative requirement containing `shall`;
- intent explaining the risk or outcome;
- implementation guidance that does not create hidden requirements;
- applicability and tailoring considerations;
- accountable control owner role;
- applicable ESAF pillars and lifecycle stages;
- expected evidence and assessment procedures;
- metrics or effectiveness indicators where meaningful;
- related controls and enhancements;
- external mappings with version and relationship metadata;
- change history and provenance.

Machine-readable metadata shall conform to `schema/control.schema.json`.

## 6. Control-writing rules

### 6.1 Atomic requirements

A control should state one primary obligation. Multiple inseparable actions may be grouped when they produce one assessable outcome. Unrelated requirements shall be separated.

### 6.2 Assessability

Requirements shall identify the responsible subject, required action or outcome, relevant object, and conditions or scope. Terms such as appropriate, adequate, regular, timely, and sufficient shall be bounded by defined criteria or tailoring guidance.

### 6.3 Technology neutrality

Controls shall describe required outcomes rather than prescribe a vendor, product, cloud, model, algorithm, or implementation unless the technology is intrinsic to the risk being controlled.

### 6.4 Guidance boundary

Implementation guidance may explain methods and examples but shall not introduce mandatory obligations absent from the requirement. Normative obligations use `shall` only in the requirement section.

### 6.5 Risk proportionality

When implementation varies by risk, the control shall identify the decision factors, required tailoring record, and minimum outcome that cannot be removed.

## 7. Lifecycle status

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| proposed | Submitted but not accepted into a working draft |
| draft | Accepted for development and review |
| approved | Approved for a planned release |
| published | Included in a published ESAF release |
| deprecated | Supported temporarily but replacement is preferred |
| retired | No longer applicable; identifier remains reserved |

Status is independent of implementation status within an adopting organization.

## 8. Applicability and tailoring

### 8.1 Applicability

Controls may apply based on capability tier, risk, lifecycle stage, data, autonomy, external exposure, affected parties, architecture, deployment model, supplier use, or obligations.

### 8.2 Tailoring actions

An adopting organization may:

- select a control into an applicable baseline;
- strengthen a control or select enhancements;
- scope implementation to defined components;
- inherit implementation from a shared service;
- substitute an equivalent or stronger control;
- mark a control not applicable with rationale;
- accept a time-bounded exception under ESAF-1000.

Tailoring shall preserve the objective, remain traceable, identify decision authority, and record residual risk.

### 8.3 Parameters

Organization-defined parameters use square brackets, for example `[organization-defined frequency]`. Parameters shall have an assigned value before implementation can be considered complete.

## 9. Baseline model

ESAF uses risk-based baselines aligned to the capability tiers in ESAF-1000.

| Baseline | Intended scope |
|---|---|
| E0 | Experimental capabilities operating within approved segregation boundaries |
| E1 | Productivity capabilities with limited material impact |
| E2 | Operational capabilities supporting recurring business processes |
| E3 | Business-critical or materially regulated capabilities |
| E4 | Mission-critical, safety-critical, or potentially severe-impact capabilities |

Baseline assignment does not replace risk assessment. Profiles may add controls or enhancements for industries, jurisdictions, architectures, or threats. Baseline selections will be defined only after sufficient controls have been approved.

## 10. Assessment model

### 10.1 Assessment objectives

Assessments evaluate both design and operating effectiveness:

- **Design effectiveness:** the control is capable of meeting its objective if operated as designed.
- **Operating effectiveness:** the control operated consistently, at the required scope and frequency, during the assessment period.

### 10.2 Assessment methods

| Method | Description |
|---|---|
| Examine | Review policies, configurations, records, logs, designs, samples, and other artifacts |
| Interview | Obtain corroborating information from accountable and knowledgeable personnel |
| Test | Exercise, reproduce, sample, query, or technically verify operation |
| Observe | Directly witness performance of a process or control |

Assessment procedures shall identify method, object, population or sample, expected result, and evidence retained.

### 10.3 Determination values

| Determination | Meaning |
|---|---|
| satisfied | Evidence supports design and operating effectiveness for the assessed scope |
| partially satisfied | Some required elements or scope are effective; gaps remain |
| not satisfied | Requirement is absent, ineffective, or unsupported by sufficient evidence |
| not applicable | Approved applicability rationale demonstrates the control does not apply |
| not assessed | The control was outside the assessment scope or evidence was not evaluated |

## 11. Evidence model

Evidence shall be evaluated for:

- **relevance:** directly supports the assessed requirement;
- **reliability:** originates from a trustworthy and controlled source;
- **completeness:** covers the required scope, period, and population;
- **timeliness:** reflects the assessed implementation and operating period;
- **integrity:** is protected from unauthorized alteration;
- **traceability:** links to the capability, control, owner, and assessment result.

Examples of evidence include approved policies, inventories, architecture decisions, configurations, access records, test results, approvals, tickets, logs, dashboards, incident records, model records, AIBOMs, training records, contracts, and audit reports.

## 12. Metrics

Metrics shall be used only when they meaningfully indicate implementation, effectiveness, risk, or outcome. A metric definition should identify its formula, data source, owner, frequency, target or threshold, limitations, and escalation path. Activity counts alone shall not be represented as effectiveness.

## 13. Control inheritance

Inherited controls shall identify:

- the provider and consuming capability;
- the implementation and responsibility boundary;
- inherited and customer-responsible elements;
- evidence and assurance available to the consumer;
- configuration dependencies and limitations;
- validation and review frequency;
- contingency when the provider changes or fails.

The accountable owner remains responsible for confirming that inherited implementation satisfies the capability's requirement.

## 14. External mapping authority

### 14.1 Authoritative records

ESAF-1600 is authoritative for external mapping assertions, taxonomy, lifecycle, review, and versioning. Every external mapping shall be maintained as an ESAF-1600 record, and control-level mapping views shall resolve from the [ESAF-1600 generated catalog](../crosswalks/CATALOG.md).

Control records remain authoritative for their requirements and versions. Their `External mappings` sections shall delegate mapping authority to ESAF-1600 rather than duplicate mapping records.

### 14.2 Interpretation boundaries

Mappings shall not be interpreted as certification, compliance, or legal advice. Proprietary source text shall not be reproduced without permission.

## 15. Catalog governance

Control proposals shall follow project governance and include technical, editorial, assessment, and mapping review appropriate to the change. Material changes create a new control version and may require a new identifier. Deprecated controls shall identify a replacement or rationale and a planned retirement release.
