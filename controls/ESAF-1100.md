## 1. Purpose

ESAF-1100 defines the control architecture used to implement and assess
[ESAF-1000](../framework/ESAF-1000.md). It establishes control families,
objectives, identifiers, required metadata, assessment methods, evidence
expectations, enhancements, baselines, and mapping semantics.

This version defines the catalog structure. It does not establish a complete
approved control baseline, approve baseline selections, or claim that every
objective has approved controls.

## 2. Control architecture

ESAF uses the following hierarchy:

```text
ESAF principle
  -> control family
    -> control objective
      -> base control
        -> control enhancement
          -> assessment procedure
            -> evidence and metrics
```

### 2.1 Principles

Principles state durable expectations established by
[ESAF-1000](../framework/ESAF-1000.md). They guide interpretation but are not
independently assessed as controls.

### 2.2 Control families

Families group related controls by enterprise capability. A family is not a
maturity level, implementation team, or organizational boundary.

### 2.3 Control objectives

Objectives define the outcome achieved by one or more controls. Objectives use
a family prefix and two-digit sequence, such as `IAM-01`. Published objectives
are listed in [OBJECTIVES.md](OBJECTIVES.md).

### 2.4 Base controls

Base controls contain one primary, assessable requirement. Controls use a
family prefix and three-digit number, such as `IAM-100`. Numbers increment by
ten to preserve space for future controls. Published controls are summarized in
[CATALOG.md](CATALOG.md) and [catalog.json](catalog.json).

### 2.5 Control enhancements

Enhancements add rigor, scope, automation, assurance, or specialized safeguards
to a base control. Enhancements do not replace the base control. They use
parenthetical numbering, such as `IAM-100(1)`.

### 2.6 Assessment procedures

Assessment procedures state how an assessor determines whether a control is
designed and operating effectively. Procedures use the control ID plus `-A` and
a sequence, such as `IAM-100-A1`. Shared assessment-result and evidence-record
fields used when recording procedure outcomes are defined in
[ESAF-1500](../assessment/ESAF-1500.md).

## 3. Identifier rules

### 3.1 Reserved patterns

| Object | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Family | Three uppercase letters | `IAM` |
| Objective | `FAM-NN` | `IAM-01` |
| Control | `FAM-NNN` | `IAM-100` |
| Enhancement | `FAM-NNN(N)` | `IAM-100(1)` |
| Assessment step | `FAM-NNN-AN` | `IAM-100-A1` |

### 3.2 Stability

Published identifiers shall not be reused for a different meaning. A retired
identifier remains reserved. Editorial changes that do not materially change
the requirement retain the identifier. A materially different requirement
receives a new identifier and records its predecessor.

### 3.3 Number allocation

Control numbers are allocated in increments of ten. Draft authors shall obtain
an unused number before opening a proposal. Numbering order does not imply
priority, maturity, or implementation sequence.

## 5. Required control content

Every proposed base control shall include:

- stable ID and title;
- family and objective;
- lifecycle status and catalog version;
- normative requirement containing `shall`;
- intent explaining the risk or outcome;
- implementation guidance that does not create hidden requirements;
- applicability and tailoring considerations;
- accountable control owner role;
- applicable ESAF pillars and lifecycle stages;
- expected evidence and assessment procedures;
- metrics or effectiveness indicators where meaningful;
- related controls and enhancements;
- external mappings with version and relationship metadata;
- change history and provenance.

Machine-readable metadata shall conform to
[`schema/control.schema.json`](schema/control.schema.json). The non-normative
authoring shape is shown in [`CONTROL_TEMPLATE.md`](CONTROL_TEMPLATE.md).

## 6. Control-writing rules

### 6.1 Atomic requirements

A control should state one primary obligation. Multiple inseparable actions may
be grouped when they produce one assessable outcome. Unrelated requirements
shall be separated.

### 6.2 Assessability

Requirements shall identify the responsible subject, required action or
outcome, relevant object, and conditions or scope. Terms such as appropriate,
adequate, regular, timely, and sufficient shall be bounded by defined criteria
or tailoring guidance.

### 6.3 Technology neutrality

Controls shall describe required outcomes rather than prescribe a vendor,
product, cloud, model, algorithm, or implementation unless the technology is
intrinsic to the risk being controlled.

### 6.4 Guidance boundary

Implementation guidance may explain methods and examples but shall not
introduce mandatory obligations absent from the requirement. Normative
obligations use `shall` only in the requirement section.

### 6.5 Risk proportionality

When implementation varies by risk, the control shall identify the decision
factors, required tailoring record, and minimum outcome that cannot be removed.

## 7. Lifecycle status

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| proposed | Submitted but not accepted into a working draft |
| draft | Accepted for development and review |
| approved | Approved for a planned release |
| published | Included in a published ESAF release |
| deprecated | Supported temporarily but replacement is preferred |
| retired | No longer applicable; identifier remains reserved |

Status is independent of implementation status within an adopting organization.

## 8. Applicability and tailoring

### 8.1 Applicability

Controls may apply based on capability tier, risk, lifecycle stage, data,
autonomy, external exposure, affected parties, architecture, deployment model,
supplier use, or obligations.

### 8.2 Tailoring actions

An adopting organization may:

- select a control into an applicable baseline;
- strengthen a control or select enhancements;
- scope implementation to defined components;
- inherit implementation from a shared service;
- substitute an equivalent or stronger control;
- mark a control not applicable with rationale;
- accept a time-bounded exception under
  [ESAF-1000](../framework/ESAF-1000.md).

Tailoring shall preserve the objective, remain traceable, identify decision
authority, and record residual risk. A `not_applicable` determination under
Section 10 shall include an approved applicability rationale consistent with
[ESAF-1500](../assessment/ESAF-1500.md).

### 8.3 Parameters

Organization-defined parameters use square brackets, for example
`[organization-defined frequency]`. Parameters shall have an assigned value
before implementation can be considered complete.

## 9. Baseline model

ESAF uses risk-based baselines aligned to the illustrative capability tiers in
[ESAF-1000](../framework/ESAF-1000.md) Appendix B.

| Baseline | Intended scope | Aligned ESAF-1000 tier |
|---|---|---|
| E0 | Experimental capabilities operating within approved segregation boundaries | Tier 0 - Experimental |
| E1 | Productivity capabilities with limited material impact | Tier 1 - Productivity |
| E2 | Operational capabilities supporting recurring business processes | Tier 2 - Operational |
| E3 | Business-critical or materially regulated capabilities | Tier 3 - Business Critical |
| E4 | Mission-critical, safety-critical, or potentially severe-impact capabilities | Tier 4 - Mission or Safety Critical |

Baseline assignment does not replace risk assessment and does not establish a
maturity level, certification result, or control determination. Profiles under
[ESAF-1800](../profiles/ESAF-1800.md) may add controls or enhancements for
industries, jurisdictions, architectures, or threats. Baseline selections will
be defined only after sufficient controls have been approved; this Working Draft
does not approve those selections.

## 10. Assessment model

ESAF-1100 is authoritative for control assessment procedures, the four
assessment methods, determination meanings, and the definitions of design and
operating effectiveness below. [ESAF-1500](../assessment/ESAF-1500.md) reuses
those methods and determination meanings without modification for shared
assessment-result records and shall remain authoritative for evidence-record,
assessment-result, finding, disposition, and maturity fields. ESAF-1100 shall
not define a maturity scale.

### 10.1 Assessment objectives

Assessments evaluate both design and operating effectiveness:

- **Design effectiveness:** the control is capable of meeting its objective if
  operated as designed.
- **Operating effectiveness:** the control operated consistently, at the
  required scope and frequency, during the assessment period.

Shared assessment-result records express those judgments using the
`design_effectiveness` and `operating_effectiveness` vocabularies defined in
[ESAF-1500](../assessment/ESAF-1500.md) (`effective`, `partially_effective`,
`ineffective`, `not_applicable`, or `not_assessed`) without replacing the
meanings above.

### 10.2 Assessment methods

| Method | Description |
|---|---|
| Examine | Review policies, configurations, records, logs, designs, samples, and other artifacts |
| Interview | Obtain corroborating information from accountable and knowledgeable personnel |
| Test | Exercise, reproduce, sample, query, or technically verify operation |
| Observe | Directly witness performance of a process or control |

Assessment procedures shall identify method, object, population or sample,
expected result, and evidence retained. When recording shared assessment
results, each method entry shall also identify the procedure and actual work
performed as required by [ESAF-1500](../assessment/ESAF-1500.md).

### 10.3 Determination values

Determination machine values match the shared ESAF-1500 assessment-result
contract. Meanings below are authoritative for ESAF-1100 and shall be reused
without modification.

| Determination | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `satisfied` | Evidence supports design and operating effectiveness for the assessed scope |
| `partially_satisfied` | Some required elements or scope are effective; gaps remain |
| `not_satisfied` | Requirement is absent, ineffective, or unsupported by sufficient evidence |
| `not_applicable` | Approved applicability rationale demonstrates the control does not apply |
| `not_assessed` | The control was outside the assessment scope or evidence was not evaluated |

A `not_applicable` determination shall include an approved applicability
rationale and approver. A `not_assessed` determination shall include a
scope-exclusion rationale. Determination values are independent of maturity
claims defined only in [ESAF-1500](../assessment/ESAF-1500.md).

## 11. Evidence model

Evidence supporting ESAF-1100 assessment procedures shall be evaluated for the
same seven quality attributes used by the shared ESAF-1500 evidence-record
contract:

- **relevance:** directly supports the assessed requirement, scope, and period;
- **reliability:** originates from a trustworthy source and collection method;
- **completeness:** covers the required scope, period, population, and facts;
- **timeliness:** reflects the assessed implementation and operating period;
- **attribution:** identifies the originating person, role, system, or
  accountable party;
- **integrity:** is protected from unauthorized alteration and can be verified;
- **traceability:** links to the capability, control, owner, procedure, and
  assessment result.

[ESAF-1500](../assessment/ESAF-1500.md) is authoritative for shared
evidence-record fields, attribute rating vocabulary, sufficiency decisions,
assessment-result records, and maturity semantics. ESAF-1100 remains
authoritative for control requirements and assessment procedures and shall not
define a parallel evidence-record schema or maturity scale.

Examples of evidence include approved policies, inventories, architecture
decisions, configurations, access records, test results, approvals, tickets,
logs, dashboards, incident records, model records, AIBOMs, training records,
contracts, and audit reports. Informative type notes appear in the Draft
[evidence catalog](../assessment/evidence-catalog/README.md).

## 12. Metrics

Metrics shall be used only when they meaningfully indicate implementation,
effectiveness, risk, or outcome. A metric definition should identify its
formula, data source, owner, frequency, target or threshold, limitations, and
escalation path. Activity counts alone shall not be represented as
effectiveness. Metric evidence, when retained as an assessment artifact, shall
use the shared ESAF-1500 evidence-record contract rather than a catalog-local
substitute.

## 13. Control inheritance

Inherited controls shall identify:

- the provider and consuming capability;
- the implementation and responsibility boundary;
- inherited and customer-responsible elements;
- evidence and assurance available to the consumer;
- configuration dependencies and limitations;
- validation and review frequency;
- contingency when the provider changes or fails.

The accountable owner remains responsible for confirming that inherited
implementation satisfies the capability's requirement.

## 14. External mapping authority

### 14.1 Authoritative records

ESAF-1600 is authoritative for external mapping assertions, taxonomy,
lifecycle, review, and versioning. The normative methodology is published at
[ESAF-1600](../crosswalks/ESAF-1600.md). Every external mapping shall be
maintained as an ESAF-1600 record, and control-level mapping views shall resolve
from the [ESAF-1600 generated catalog](../crosswalks/CATALOG.md).

Control records remain authoritative for their requirements and versions. Their
`External mappings` sections shall delegate mapping authority to ESAF-1600
rather than duplicate mapping records.

### 14.2 Interpretation boundaries

Mappings shall not be interpreted as certification, compliance, or legal
advice. Proprietary source text shall not be reproduced without permission.

## 15. Catalog governance

Control proposals shall follow project governance and include technical,
editorial, assessment, and mapping review appropriate to the change. Material
changes create a new control version and may require a new identifier.
Deprecated controls shall identify a replacement or rationale and a planned
retirement release.
